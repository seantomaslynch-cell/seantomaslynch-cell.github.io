How to use the summary and the cards

Page one is a summary table with one row per episode, for a quick overview. Page two has a card for the details of each episode, and you can print it as many times as you need. Fill in the date and start time first, because those are the details that fade fastest. If you can do it safely, glance at a clock or start a timer on your phone when it begins. Then tick everything you saw, and use the Other line for anything the list misses. Write how your dog acted afterward, and roughly how long it took before they seemed like themselves again. A best guess is fine. Just mark it as a guess.

Why a summary and cards

Episodes are different from each other, and a small table cell does not leave much room to describe what happened. The summary table gives you the overview at a glance, and each card gives you space to write a few plain sentences and keeps one episode together. Clip the cards together in date order, and your vet can flip through them like a short history. If you would rather see every episode as one row, the general printable seizure log uses a single table and works for dogs and cats.

Note what came before, without deciding what it means

Write down what was going on in the hour before: a meal, exercise, a visitor, a loud noise, a car ride, or nothing at all. The box for medication that was late or missed is there because your vet will want to know. You are collecting facts. Your vet decides which ones matter, so try not to draw conclusions yourself, and never change a medication because of what the cards seem to show.

Bring every card to the vet

Take all of the cards, your dog's current medication list, and a short video if you were able to take one safely. Never put yourself or your dog at risk to get a video, because the written card is enough on its own. The vet visit checklist lists what else to bring, and a medication log shows which doses were given, and when.

Common questions

Can I use this for a cat?

The fields describe things you can observe in any pet, so it works. The general seizure log is written for both dogs and cats.

Does this tell me what kind of seizure my dog had?

No. It only records what you saw. Your vet may use the cards to understand what is happening, and decides what it means.

I could not time it. Is the card still useful?

Yes. Write "not sure" and your best guess, and mark it as an estimate. Being honest about what you know is more useful to your vet than a precise-looking number that is really a guess.

Where should the cards live?

Keep them in one place that everyone in the household knows about, so whoever sees an episode can fill one in. Ask them to add their initials in the notes.

About this template

Print it as often as you like. Leave anything you do not need blank. Fill in the top fields by hand once, then copy the sheet for later weeks.
