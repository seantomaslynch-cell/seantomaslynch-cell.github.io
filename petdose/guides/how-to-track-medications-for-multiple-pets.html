How to track medications for more than one pet

One pet on one medication is easy to keep in your head. Two or three pets on different medications, with different people helping, is where mix-ups start. Bottles look alike, names sound alike, and everyone assumes someone else did it. This guide covers a simple system that keeps each pet's medications separate. It is about record keeping. Your vet decides every medication, amount and time.

If you would like a ready-made sheet, there is a free printable weekly medication chart.

Give every pet their own line

Whether you use paper or an app, each pet needs a separate line. On a weekly chart, that means one row for every pet and medication, with the pet's name in the first column. If you keep a running log instead, add a Pet column to each entry, or keep one sheet per pet. What you want to avoid is a shared list of entries that does not say which animal it was for.

Store the medications separately too

Keep each pet's medications in their own labeled bag or container, in the original packaging, with the pharmacy label showing. Write the pet's name in large letters on a piece of tape on the outside, without covering the pharmacy label. Store the containers in the same spot so that there is only one place to look. Get rid of old bottles you no longer use so they cannot be picked up by mistake.

Read the label out loud, every time

Similar pills, similar names and similar-looking pets are exactly how the wrong medication ends up with the wrong animal. Before you give anything, read the pet's name and the medication name on the label, and then check the chart. It takes five seconds, and it catches the mistakes that tired people make in the morning.

Feed apart when medication is in the food

If a pill is hidden in a meal and the pets eat side by side, one may finish the other's portion. Feed each pet in a separate spot, and stay until the food is gone, so you know who ate what. Then write it down.

One place to look, one habit to follow

Put the chart on the fridge or beside the medications, with a pen tied to it. Ask everyone in the home to check it before giving a dose, and to write the time and their initials right after. If a dose is missed or you are not sure it was given, do not guess and do not make it up later. Call your vet, and read our guide on what to do if you think a dose was doubled or missed.

Track refills on the same page

With several pets, medicines run out on different days. Write each refill date on the same calendar you use for vet visits, and check the bottles once a week. When you leave your pets with someone, a pet sitter sheet and a copy of the chart tell them exactly what each animal gets. Our guide on preparing a pet sitter has more on handoffs.

Common questions

Should I use one sheet per pet or one for the whole house?

Either works, as long as every entry names the pet. One sheet for the whole house is quicker to check. One sheet per pet is easier to hand to a vet.

What if two people give medications at the same time?

Give each person their own rows, or ask them to write their initials next to each entry. The point is that every dose has a time and a name.

Do I need to record the amounts?

You can copy them from the label if you wish, but never work them out yourself. Your vet's instructions are the source of truth.
