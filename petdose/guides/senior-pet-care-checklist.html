Senior pet care checklist

Older pets often need a little more attention: more vet visits, more medications, and more small changes to keep track of. This page is a checklist you can print, plus a short guide to using it. It is not medical advice, and it does not say what is normal for an older pet. Your vet decides that. Its job is to help you keep your paperwork in order, notice things, and arrive at appointments with clear notes.

How the checklist works

There are four parts. The first part is paperwork: a single medication list, the vet's phone numbers, and the date of the next check-up. The second is the house: small changes that make daily life easier for an older animal. The third is a list of things to notice, and the fourth is a check-in table where you write a few words on a regular date. Tick a box when it is done, and come back to the page whenever something changes. You do not need to do everything at once.

Notice, write it down, tell your vet

The most useful thing you can bring to a senior visit is a dated list of what you saw. "She stopped jumping on the couch sometime in March" gives your vet far more to work with than "she is slowing down." Try not to explain the changes to yourself, and do not treat anything on your own. Write what you saw in plain words, then let your vet interpret it. The check-in log gives you a small place to do that each month, or on whatever schedule works for you.

Keep the medication list in one place

Older pets are more likely to take several medications, sometimes at different times of day. One current list, with each name copied from the label, saves a lot of confusion at the vet's office and for anyone helping at home. The medication log records each dose as it is given, and the weekly medication chart puts a whole week on one page. Whenever a medication changes, update the list the same day.

Bring it to appointments

Take the page, your medication records and the recent weights to each visit. The vet visit checklist lists the rest of what to bring, and a weight log shows how the numbers have moved over time. Ask your vet how often they would like to see your pet, and write the next date in the checklist so it does not slip.

Common questions

At what age is my pet a senior?

It depends on the species, the breed and the size of your pet. Your vet can tell you when your pet is considered a senior, and what that means for their care.

How often should I fill in the check-in log?

Choose a routine you can keep, such as the same day each month, and ask your vet whether they would like it more or less often. A short entry you actually write beats a detailed one you skip.

Does the checklist tell me if something is wrong?

No. It only helps you record what you notice. If you are worried about your pet, call your vet.
